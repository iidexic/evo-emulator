# E007: the standard world over 250,000 ticks (2026-10-07)

Status: design and predictions written 2026-10-07 before any run longer
than the 50,000-tick E004 runs of the same world. No E007 run had been
started when this section was written.

Bears on: PLAN §10 (immunity and arms races are expected at desktop
scale), the E005 result that parasites exist in the standard world, the
E006 decision to keep `self` = executor because parasites are the raw
material for defences, and H5 (upkeep plus decay give enough turnover).

## Question

Every run so far stops at tick 50,000, about 40 generations of the
median replicator in the standard world. That is enough to see
parasites arise (E005: 50 lineages per run) but not enough to see
whether anything happens to them. Over 250,000 ticks on the same
physics:

1. Does replication persist, or does bit rot plus small populations
   grind the world down?
2. Do hosts change in a way that makes the parasites' trick stop
   working (resistance), or does parasitism stay a free ride?
3. Does the dominant genome change over time (sweeps), and does its
   length drift?

The physics argument for question 2, written down before the runs: a
fall-through parasite runs the host's copy loop with its own energy and
writes into its own `alloc` region; the host's bytes are write-protected
and the host's energy is untouched. The parasite costs the host nothing
the host could be selected against, except space in the patch. A host
prefix that trapped intruders (an intruder arrives with registers set by
its own code, a newborn host with registers zeroed, so `skipz D ; trap`
would separate them) needs the host's own restart to skip the prefix,
several coordinated mutations with no payoff until all are in place.
The prediction below is that it does not happen within 250,000 ticks.

## World and runs

The standard world, which is the code default since 2026-10-07 (E004
p512_far_prop_rot: 128 patches of 512 bytes at 8x sun, far `alloc`,
proportional absorb, bit rot 1e-4, `self` = executor). One arm, `std`,
no flags. Seeds 1–10, 250,000 ticks, census every 500 (500 world
snapshots per run), recorded and classified as E004 did; runner
`analysis/e007_long_run.py`, output `runs/e007/std/s<seed>/`. The first
50,000 ticks of each run reproduce the E004 p512_far_prop_rot run of the
same seed (same seed, same defaults, determinism checked byte-for-byte
in E004's defaults flip), so seed 10, which lost its founder early in
E004, is expected to do the same here.

Harness classes come from `classes.csv` (the default physics, which is
the world's own physics here, so E005's `classes_world.csv` is the same
file). Parasite lineages use E005's 2+-children test over the whole run:
dependent (world-replicating, harness class not `replicator`), median
foreign-execution share over carriers with 2+ children above 0.25,
generation depth ≥ 5.

New tool: `evo-core --host HEX --host-body HEX` runs the two-genome
harness with any host genome in place of the ancestor (checked: the
ancestor's bytes as `--host-body` reproduce the plain `--host` result).

## Measures

Per run, in ten windows of 25,000 ticks (window w covers ticks
(25,000·(w−1), 25,000·w]; window 2 is ticks 25,000–50,000, the second
half of an E004 run):

- population, replicator-class bodies and occupied patches at the last
  census of the window; births and exact share in the window.
- the **top host** of the window: the replicator-class genome with the
  most exact births in the window; its hash, length and share of the
  window's exact births. Genotypes with ≥ 2 exact births in the window.
- dependent, parasite and trampoline (dependent with foreign share
  below 0.05 over 2+-children carriers) shares of the window's exact
  births; parasite genomes active in the window; parasite genomes whose
  first exact birth falls in the window.
- **resistance**: the run's main parasite (most exact births over the
  whole run) in the harness before the window's top host, 3,000 quiet
  ticks: its exact births, and the host's exact births with it there.
  The same parasite before the ancestor gives the baseline (E005: 108–180
  exact children for the 21-byte parasites, 340 for the one-byte body).
  Resistance ratio = exact before the window's host / exact before the
  ancestor.
- also: the window's own top parasite before the window's top host.

Whole-run: E004's persistence table (`replicating` = exact births by
replicator-class parents in the last 5,000 ticks) and E005's parasite
table.

## Predictions

1. Persistence: replicating at tick 250,000 in at least 7 of 10 seeds
   (E004 at 50,000: 9 of 10). Population at the end a median of
   700–1,100 (E004: 939). Bit rot at 1e-4 flips about two bytes per
   body-lifetime; the population recovers from that at 50,000 and
   there is no reason it stops.
2. Parasite lineages keep arising: in at least 8 of the surviving
   seeds, at least one parasite genome has its first exact birth after
   tick 200,000. Fall-through parasitism is one broken restart away
   from the ancestor, so it recurs by mutation.
3. Parasitism stays a minor, stable share: the median across seeds of
   the parasite share of exact births is between 0.1% and 3% in every
   window from the second on (E005 whole-run: 0.7%), and no seed-window
   is above 10%.
4. No resistance: in at least 8 of the surviving seeds the resistance
   ratio in the last window is at least 0.5, and the median ratio across
   seeds in the last window is within 0.5–1.5 of the median in window 2.
5. Length holds: in at least 8 of the surviving seeds the top host of
   the last window is 18–24 bytes long (the ancestor is 21). Upkeep per
   byte pulls down, store cap per byte pulls up, and no stronger force
   is known.
6. Sweeps happen: in the median seed the top host changes between
   consecutive windows at least 3 times out of 9. With rot 1e-4 and
   write noise, a dominant genotype of a few hundred bodies does not
   hold for 200 generations.
7. Diversity is stationary: the median across seeds of genotypes with
   ≥ 2 exact births in the last window is within a factor of 2 of the
   same number in window 2.
8. Exploratory, no threshold: trampoline share per window; the tick of
   extinction where it happens; median age at the end; the host's exact
   births in the harness with the parasite present, against the
   ancestor's with the same parasite.

Calibration note, added after the runs were launched and before any
E007 file was read: the window analysis was checked on the E004
p512_far_prop_rot seed-1 run (50,000 ticks, two windows). There the
main parasite (the one-byte `pad`) makes 340 exact children before the
ancestor but 202 and 187 before the top hosts of windows 1 and 2, a
ratio of 0.59 and 0.55 with no time for resistance to evolve: the top
host of a window is some mutant of the ancestor, not the ancestor, and
its loop constants differ. So the absolute threshold in prediction 4
(≥ 0.5) sits near the no-change baseline and the informative part of
prediction 4 is the last-window-to-window-2 comparison. The same check
showed the top host holds about 2% of a window's exact births, with
about 3,700 genotypes having ≥ 2 exact births per window, so
prediction 6 (sweeps) is counting changes among near-ties; its result
will be reported with the top host's share beside it.

If 4 holds, hosts have no answer to parasites in this physics because
parasites cost them nothing, and Phase 3 should make parasitism costly
(a drain op, or children that take the host's energy) before expecting
immunity (PLAN §10). If 4 fails, the top host genomes of the late
windows are read for the mechanism and that is the first evolved
defence in the project. If 1 fails, the standard world is not stable
over the long run and the rot rate or patch economy is the next thing
to look at.

### What the top hosts are

Not part of the pre-registered analysis: the 90 top-host genomes were
read against the ancestor during review (2026-10-07), and what they
showed was then checked over the whole population, in the harness, and
in a world without parasites.

- **Every top host from window 2 on has a longer absorb loop.** The
  ancestor's `lit A 64` before `absorb ; dec A ; skipz A ; jmpr D` is
  `lit A 106`–`lit A 127` in 80 of the 81 seed-windows from window 2
  on; the one exception (seed 3, window 3) is `lit A -114 ; inc A`,
  which absorbs 114 times by the other route. In window 1 it is 96–118
  in seven seeds and the ancestor's 64 in seeds 3 and 8. The other
  differences from the ancestor are flips of bit 7 in instruction
  bytes, which the decoder does not read for a register operand (bits
  0–4 pick the op, bits 5–6 the register), so they leave the
  disassembly unchanged, and changes to the `lit A 16` endowment before
  `divide`.
- **It is a sweep of the whole population, finished inside window 1.**
  Over all replicator-class exact births of 21-byte bodies, the median
  absorb count is 72 at tick 2,500, 96 at 5,000, 112 at 10,000 and 120
  at 25,000 (medians across the 9 seeds of per-seed medians). The
  ancestor's 64 is below 1% of births from tick 7,500 in every seed and
  gone by 22,500. From window 2 to 10 the median sits at 120–123 and
  the 90th percentile at 125–127. The `lit` operand is a signed byte,
  so 127 is the largest count the loop can encode (128 or more is
  negative, and a `dec` loop from a negative 32-bit register does not
  end); whether the population is held against that ceiling is tested
  in the follow-up below, and it is not. The same sweep is in E004's
  p512_far_prop_rot runs, which are the first 50,000 ticks of these,
  and went unnoticed there.
- **The absorb count alone produces the resistance ratio.** The
  ancestor with byte 6 changed to 124 and nothing else, in the harness
  with `pad` before it, gives 183 parasite children and 140 host
  children, the medians of windows 2–10 (64: 340 and 211; 96: 234 and
  167; 112: 202 and 148; 127: 180 and 137). The parasite's yield falls
  further than the host's (0.54 against 0.66) because its cycle is the
  host's absorb loop plus a one-byte copy, about 4N + 14 instructions
  per child against about 4N + 94 for the host, so a longer absorb
  phase is a larger share of it.
- **The sweep is not parasite-driven.** Three seeds of the same world
  with `--self-owner` (E006's physics, under which an intruder copies
  the host and no fall-through parasite lineage forms), 25,000 ticks,
  run during this review: the median absorb count is 112 by tick 10,000
  and 112–120 at 25,000, the same course. What the longer absorb phase
  buys the host in the crowded, proportional-absorb world is not
  measured here; it is bought with or without parasites.
- **The three near-zero ratios are the endowment, not a defence.**
  Those top hosts carry `lit A 85`, `lit A 86` and `lit A 37` before
  `divide` (ancestor: 16). `divide` gives the child min(A, energy −
  the divide cost); a one-byte `pad` holds at most 64 units, so before
  a host whose endowment is about 35 or more it gives its first child
  nearly everything and starves, and the harness stops when the
  intruder dies. The ancestor with only this byte changed to 35 gives 2
  parasite children; 34 gives 340. In the world such a host lets a
  `pad` make one child before it dies, replacement at best. The
  endowment byte drifts with a widening upper tail: the share of
  replicator-class exact births with endowment ≥ 37 is 5% in window 1
  and 13–15% in windows 9–10 (medians; 32% in the highest seed-window),
  and in seed 9 from window 7 on most births set A before `divide` by
  other instructions than `lit` (`self 0 ; swap B`, `load A`). Whether
  parasites select for a large endowment or newborn survival does
  (newborns are 24% of replicator deaths) is not separable here; the
  cost to `pad` is a side effect either way, and 85–90% of hosts do not
  carry it.
- **Raw genotype counts overstate the diversity.** Counting canonical
  genomes (`func_hash`, which drops the unread modifier bits) instead
  of raw bytes, the genotypes with ≥ 2 exact births in a window fall
  from 3,500–3,900 to 2,050–2,550, and the leading canonical genotype
  holds 5–13% of the window's exact births instead of 1.5–4.6%. The
  cloud is real but smaller than the prediction-6 and -7 numbers say,
  and it sits on one phenotype, not on the ancestor.

### What this says

- **H12 supported on its registered test, with the baseline restated.**
  From window 2 to window 10 the parasite's yield before the hosts of
  each window is flat at 0.53–0.55 of its yield before the ancestor, in
  every seed, and nothing else about the host–parasite relation moved
  over 200,000 ticks. The drop from 1 to 0.55 is not the generic
  difference between the ancestor and its mutants, as the calibration
  note supposed: it is the longer absorb loop that swept every seed in
  the first 10,000 ticks, which the parasite inherits by running the
  host's code, and which happens with parasitism switched off. It is a
  change in the hosts that costs the parasites something, but it is not
  a defence: it is not parasite-driven, it costs the host a third of
  its own harness yield, and the parasites' share of births barely
  moved (1.2% in window 1, 0.8–1.0% after).
- **Selection is fast enough here; a defence did not come.** The absorb
  count went from 64 to 112–127 in about 10,000 ticks in 12 of 12 runs
  (9 here, 3 self-owner). The 250,000 ticks without a
  defence are therefore not for want of selective power. The
  pre-registered branch for "4 holds" applies: the parasites cost the
  hosts nothing the hosts can be selected against, and immunity should
  not be expected before parasitism is made costly (PLAN §10).
- **The standard world is stationary after window 1.** Population,
  replicator count, diversity, exact share, dependent share, parasite
  share and trampoline share are flat across windows 2–10, and
  parasites are reinvented at about E005's rate per tick (171–228
  lineages per run against 50 per 50,000 ticks). Bit rot does not grind
  the world down (H5). Length holds at 21 bytes in every window of
  every seed.
- **One sweep, then a cloud.** No genotype takes over: the leading
  canonical genotype holds 5–13% of a window's exact births and the
  leader changes every window. But the cloud sits on one phenotype
  (absorb count 112–127 in 97–99% of replicator births from window 2
  on), not on the ancestor. Prediction 6's measure counts leader
  changes among synonymous and near-synonymous variants and could not
  have registered the sweep that did happen; about 40% of prediction
  7's diversity is silent variation. The endowment byte is the one
  thing still moving, slowly, and in seed 9 it has moved off `lit`
  altogether.

It is drift, not a sweep.

- **No common direction.** From window 1 to 10 the seed median rose
  in 3 seeds, fell in 4 and ended where it began in 2; seeds 1, 2, 3
  and 6 climbed to 24–26 and came back.
- **The seeds diverge.** Seeds 1–8 span 16–18 in window 1 and 13–30
  from window 8 (standard deviation 0.7, then 3.4–5.0), with no
  reconvergence.
- **Only the spread moves everywhere.** The ancestor's 16 falls from
  57% to 0.1%; the 10th and 90th percentiles go from 16 and 26 to 11
  and 42. The ≥ 37 tail grows because the distribution widens.
- **Seed 9 changed the mechanism.** Byte 16 is `self 0` in under 0.4%
  of births before window 6, then 20, 49, 65, 74 and 78%. `self 0`
  sets B to the body length, and `self 0 ; swap B ; divide A` and
  `self 0 ; divide B` both endow 21, whatever byte 17 holds. The 37–38
  medians describe the other 22–26%. One seed; selection and hitchhiking
  are not separable here.

### Follow-up: what the longer absorb loop buys, and the ceiling (2026-10-07)

Twelve 25,000-tick runs of the standard world seeded with the ancestor
at K = 64, 100, 120 and 127 (`--k`), seeds 1–3 each, classified as
usual; the K = 64 runs are byte-identical to the first 25,000 ticks of
E007 seeds 1–3 (runs, scripts and the full report in
`runs/e007_followup/k/`; the three self-owner runs of the section above
are in `runs/e007_followup/self_owner/`). Absorb count here includes the
two other loop forms that replicate (`lit A -N ; inc A`, counted as N; and a deleted `absorb`
whose loop re-enters on byte 6, counted as the byte).

What K = 120 does not buy, against K = 64 in the first 5,000 ticks:
cycles are slower (20 ticks between divisions against 15–17, first
division at 21 against 16), newborn deaths are not fewer (0.17–0.19 of
replicator deaths against 0.15–0.16), living bodies hold no more energy,
colonisation is slower (60–70 patches at tick 1,000 against 87–95), and
alone in the quiet harness it makes 149 children against 230. What it
buys is reproduction on a drawn-down pool: a cycle costs about 165 + 4K
units plus endowment and upkeep, so the ration a body needs to break
even falls from about 7.4 units at K = 64 to 6.0 at K = 120 (an
estimate from the cost table, not a measurement). Once the world is
full the K = 120 arm holds pools lower (2.84–2.89M against 3.04–3.07M
per patch), absorbs more (123.5–124.1M against 119.4–121.3M per patch
per 500 ticks), overflows less, and makes slightly more replicator
exact births per tick at the same population. Within one run and bin
during the sweep, bodies with K = 120–123 leave 1.10–1.21 times the
mean number of exact children and bodies with K = 64 leave 0.92–0.95;
with K ≥ 112, 0.22–0.27 of bodies get at least one exact child against
0.17–0.21 at K = 65–95. These are lineages that differ in other bytes
too, so the comparison is correlational.

The ceiling does not bind. Medians of the absorb count over
replicator-class exact births, last bin (ticks 22,500–25,000):

|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 120 | 120 | 122 |
| 100 | 117 | 116 | 122 |
| 120 | 122 | 120 | 120 |
| 127 | 126 | 124 | 120 |

From 127 the median falls and the share of births at exactly 127 drops
from 0.69 / 0.73 / 0.39 in the first bin to 0.43 / 0.35 / 0.12 in the
last, at least as fast as neutral loss would predict (97.5% of children
of a 127 parent keep 127; about 130 generations in 25,000 ticks).
Relative fitness within the K = 127 runs is 0.99–1.03 for every group
from 116 to 127, 0.74–1.00 for 96–111, 0.50–0.92 for 65–95 and
0.13–0.56 below 64. So the landscape is flat from about 112 up, and the
standard world's 120 is not an optimum but where the largest single-bit
steps from 64 land: setting bits 5, 4 and 3 gives 96, 112 and 120, the
sweep's medians at ticks 5,000, 10,000 and 25,000, and the gain from
each further bit is too small to see (the break-even ration drops 1.0
units from 64 to 96 and 0.09 from 120 to 127). Bodies past the ceiling
(a negative byte 6 with `dec`) do not replicate: 0.04–0.10% of the
children of K = 127 parents, all loafers or dead in the harness.

### Caveats

- 10 seeds, one founder per run; 9 surviving seeds carry every median.
- Resistance is measured with one parasite (the run's main one, `pad`
  in every seed) against one host per window, in a 3,000-tick quiet
  harness. A defence that worked only against other parasite genomes,
  or only in the crowded world, would not show. The window's own top
  parasite is tested too and never does better than `pad`.
- "Top host" is the most exact births among replicator-class genomes,
  which at ~2% share is a weak notion of dominance. Prediction 6 is
  confirmed but does not measure what it was meant to. Predictions 6
  and 7 count raw genotypes; bit-7 flips in register operands are
  silent, so `func_hash` is the unit to use next time, and a sweep
  measure should track a trait (a byte or a loop constant), not a
  leader.
- The sweep reading is post hoc: the trait was found by reading the
  top-host genomes after the pre-registered checks. The harness
  decomposition (byte 6 alone reproduces the window medians) is exact;
  the self-owner comparison is 3 seeds × 25,000 ticks run during the
  review, not a pre-registered arm.
- The resistance harness stops when the intruder dies, so a host that
  starves the intruder at its first division records a ratio near 0
  whatever happens over 3,000 ticks. Three seed-windows are of this
  kind; no last-window host is, so prediction 4's check is unaffected.
- The Linux rerun matched Windows to tick 20,000 only. Beyond that,
  bit-identity between machines is untested; the result does not
  depend on it.
