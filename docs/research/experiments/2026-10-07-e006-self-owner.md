# E006: the `self` switch (2026-10-07)

Status: design and predictions written 2026-10-07 before any E006 run
beyond one 20,000-tick smoke run at seed 1 (used only to check that the
switch runs and the population persists: 900 organisms at tick 20,000).

Bears on: H8 (`self` semantics change the ecology, PLAN §9), and the
E005 result that parasitism in the E004 world is a side effect of `self`
naming the executor.

## Question

E005 found that every parasite lineage in the E004 world works the same
way: a body whose IP runs off its end into a replicator gets copied by
that replicator's loop, because `self` returns the executing organism's
start and length. What happens when `self` names the living owner of the
byte at IP instead, so the host's loop run by an intruder copies the
host?

## Change under test

`--self-owner` (`Config.self_owner`, default off): `self` with modifier 0
sets A and B to the start and length of the living organism that owns
the byte at IP. On a free or debris byte, or inside the executor's own
body or pending region, it names the executor as before. Modifiers 1 (A
= IP) and 2 (fail flag) are unchanged.

Analog: an enzyme acts on the substrate it is bound to, not on the
molecule that produced it. Tierra's parasites and hyper-parasites arose
from this rule: a host's copy procedure copied whichever organism's
registers pointed at it.

What the two-genome harness says before the runs (`--host`, quiet E004
physics, 3,000 ticks):

| body before an ancestor | rule | its exact births | host copies it made | host's own exact births |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21 pads | executor | 214 | 0 | 141 |
| 21 pads | owner | 0 | 125 | 105 |
| 1 pad | executor | 340 | 0 | 211 |
| 1 pad | owner | 0 (dies after 285 instructions) | 0 | – |

A one-byte body cannot run a host cycle under the owner rule: its store
cap is 64 energy (64 per body byte) and one 21-byte host cycle costs
about 370, so it starves inside the host's absorb loop. Under the
executor rule its cycle copied one byte and cost about 260 with the
absorb loop refilling it.

## Arms

Two arms with `--self-owner`, seeds 1–10, 50,000 ticks, census every
500, recorded and classified as E004 did (`analysis/e006_self_owner.py`,
output `runs/e006/`):

| arm | flags | control (E004 runs, same seeds) |
|---|---|---|
| owner_prop_rot | `--patch 512 --sun 8 --alloc-far --absorb-prop --rot 1e-4 --self-owner` | p512_far_prop_rot |
| owner | `--patch 512 --sun 8 --alloc-far --self-owner` | p512_far |

Measures: E004's persistence table (replicating at the end, population,
classes, newborn share) and E005's parasite analysis (dependent share,
parasite lineages by the 2+-children test, mechanism breakdown,
other-source births, host harness on the main parasite).

## Predictions

1. Persistence unchanged: replicating at tick 50,000 in at least 8 of 10
   seeds in both arms (controls 9 and 10 of 10). Hosts lose nothing by
   the switch; intruders' energy now goes into host copies.
2. Parasite lineages (dependent, not a replicator under world physics,
   depth ≥ 5, median foreign share over carriers with 2+ children above
   0.25) fall to a median below 5 per run in both arms (controls 50 and
   32), and their share of exact births after tick 10,000 falls below
   0.1% (controls 0.7% and 0.8%).
3. The dependent share of exact births after tick 10,000 falls to less
   than half the control's (controls 0.111 and 0.084): the broken-restart
   variants lose their second child per cycle and survive only on their
   own loop at replacement rate 1.
4. Births whose copy source is another living organism rise above 2% of
   all births (controls 0.4% and 0.5%), and more than half of them are
   exact copies of the source's birth genome (controls 0.1–0.4%).
   Intruders copying hosts show up exactly there.
5. No genome of 1 or 2 bytes has 5 or more exact births after tick
   10,000 in any seed (E004: a 1–2-byte genome was the main parasite in 7
   of 19 proportional-absorb runs). The store cap forbids it.
6. Dead-code trampolines are unaffected, because `self` on free bytes
   still names the executor: dependent genomes with foreign share below
   0.05 keep a share of all exact births within a factor of 2 of the
   control's (about 0.02).
7. Exploratory, no threshold: population at the end and replicator-class
   count at the end, against the controls (939 and 964; 249 and 178).

If 2–4 hold, H8 is supported: the same physics with one ISA rule flipped
turns parasites into donors. If parasites persist at the control rate,
the mechanism in E005 was misread and the record says so. If
persistence fails (1), the switch costs hosts something unforeseen and
the runs are read for what.

### Predictions

1. Persistence unchanged, ≥ 8 of 10: **confirmed**, 9 and 10 of 10,
   population and class counts within 2–8% of the controls.
2. Parasite lineages below 5 per run and below 0.1% of exact births:
   **refuted as thresholded**, but the direction holds: 20 and 12 per
   run (from 50 and 32), 0.2% of exact births (from 0.7–0.8%). The
   remaining "parasites" by the 2+-children test are not host-mediated:
   in the two-genome harness every main parasite makes 0–2 exact
   children before an ancestor (E005's made 60–180), and 91% of their
   exact births in the world are copies of their own body by their own
   loop. Their foreign share comes from falling into hosts and copying
   the host, which under this rule is a donation, not a birth of their
   own. The test's threshold was calibrated on the executor rule, where
   foreign execution meant self-copying; here it means host-copying.
3. Dependent share below half the control: **refuted as thresholded**,
   0.076 from 0.111 (−32%) and 0.048 from 0.084 (−43%).
4. Other-source births above 2%, more than half exact copies of the
   source: **first half confirmed** (4.1% and 3.9%, an 8–10× rise),
   **second half refuted** (0.38 and 0.21). The copies that are not
   exact are host copies made with the intruder's register state, which
   the host's prologue does not fully reset (a `swap` sequence that
   assumes zeroed registers).
5. No 1–2-byte genome with ≥ 5 exact births: **refuted**, 39 and 15
   such genomes. But they reproduce in a different way than in E005:
   their carriers spend 98–99% of instructions in unowned memory and
   under 2% in living bodies, so they run dead replicator code, where
   `self` still names the executor. Their chains are shorter (deepest 4
   and 2, against 8 and 5), and the one-byte body that E005 found
   before living hosts is gone, as the store-cap argument said.
6. Dead-code trampolines within a factor of 2 of the control:
   **confirmed**, 0.013 and 0.016 against 0.018 and 0.021.
7. Exploratory, hosts gain: **no**. Replicator-class bodies at the end
   231 and 173 against 249 and 178; population 914 and 966 against 939
   and 964. The donated copies (4% of births) do not show up as more
   hosts; they are made by broken bodies in drained patches and die as
   newborns like any other child there.

### What this says

- **H8 supported in kind, not in degree.** One ISA rule flipped and the
  parasitism of E005 is gone: no lineage reproduces through a living
  host's code (host harness 0–2 against 60–180). In its place is a
  donor economy: 4% of all births are copies of a living host made by
  a broken body that fell into it, paid for with the intruder's energy.
  Tierra's hyper-parasite trick is exactly this rule turned to the
  host's advantage; here no host has yet evolved to exploit it, and
  the hosts gain nothing measurable from the gifts.
- **Dead code is the constant.** Both rules leave free memory full of
  dead replicators, and both the trampolines (unchanged, prediction 6)
  and the 1–2-byte lineages (now entirely dead-code driven) live on it.
  Under the owner rule, dead bodies are the only code an intruder can
  use to copy itself. This is a physics fact about conserved matter
  with decaying ownership (PLAN §5.1, §5.7), independent of `self`.
- **The dependent class is mostly not about neighbours at all.** It fell
  by a third, not to zero, and world-physics classes never explain it.
  The remainder is one-shot replicators (own loop, broken restart, one
  child per carrier) plus the dead-code users above. The harness
  threshold of 2 exact children hides one-shot replicators as
  `inexact`; a class for them would make the dependent table smaller
  and more honest.

### Caveats

- 10 seeds per arm, one founder per run, same seeds as the controls but
  a different binary (the controls were recorded at 1cc5d21 with
  uncommitted switches; the defaults are byte-identical, checked in
  E004).
- The 2+-children foreign-share test is kept for comparability but no
  longer measures parasitism under the owner rule; the host harness
  does.
- "Donation" is inferred from executor class and the harness, not from
  tracing individual intruders.
- No world snapshots, so the dead code the 1–2-byte lineages run is
  inferred from the unowned-execution counters.

### Decision

2026-10-07: keep `self` = executing organism as the default. The owner
rule removes the only organism-to-organism interaction the world has
produced so far and replaces it with a gift nobody uses; the executor
rule gives parasites, which is the Phase 2 criterion and the raw
material for immunity and arms races (PLAN §10). The switch stays as a
flag for Phase 4 ISA comparisons. The code defaults now move to the
E004 world (PLAN §8 decision), with the E001–E003 world behind a flag.
