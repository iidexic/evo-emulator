# E012: the standard world without write protection (2026-10-12)

Status: design and predictions written 2026-10-12, before any
`--no-protect` run of the standard world. The flag has existed since
Phase 1 and has never been run in this world (gap review 2026-10-09,
§1). Two numbers from the E007 control were read while designing this
and are quoted below: `writes_blocked` summed over the deaths of
seeds 1 and 2 to tick 100,000.

Bears on: H4 (write protection is necessary for coexistence,
2026-09-30), E005 and E007 (with protection on, parasites and hosts
coexist, parasites 0.8–1.0% of exact births).

## Question

H4 predicts that with protection off the seeded population collapses
to a single fast overwriter and no two genotypes coexist for long
(Coreworld broke within about 200 iterations; Amoeba-IV never held two
species). With protection on, parasites and hosts coexist; E005–E007
showed that much. Does the standard world collapse without protection?

## What protection does here, written before the runs

`--no-protect` sets `write_protection = false`: a `store` or `copy` by
any organism may land on any byte within its locality radius, including
another living organism's body or pending region. Ownership is not
transferred: the bytes stay the victim's, and the victim goes on
executing them.

The copy target in the ancestor's code comes from `alloc`, which only
grants free or debris bytes, so a working replicator never aims at a
neighbour. What protection refuses in the standard world is the
collateral: copy loops that run past their region after a slip, copy
loops whose `alloc` failed with A still in range, and `store`s by
broken bodies. E007 counts these as `writes_blocked`: 132,578 (seed 1)
and 176,908 (seed 2) over the deaths to tick 100,000, about 1.3–1.8
per tick per world, from about 0.8% of the organisms that died
(9,000 and 7,847), against 31 million copies. Spread over the ~20,000
owned bytes of a full world that is close to the bit-rot rate per byte
(1e-4), but clumped: about 17 writes per writer, so a few victims each
take a run of bytes.

An overwriter in Coreworld's sense, one that reproduces by writing its
own code over a neighbour, gains nothing here: the victim keeps the
bytes and its own store, the writer pays 3 units per `copy` and gets no
child out of it, and `alloc` already gives every replicator private
room. The prediction is that protection off adds damage and changes
nothing structural.

New counters (2026-10-12, `docs/instrumentation.md`):
`writes_foreign`, writes by an organism that landed on another living
organism's bytes, and `overwritten`, the same writes counted on the
victim. Tested: the two sum to the same total over a world, and both
are 0 with protection on. The default world is unchanged by the
counters (the 20,000-tick seed-1 check of E007 gives the same
`census.csv` and `births.csv` hashes, `6f523da9f7e05a4e` and
`33b07ce87cc80ea6`; `deaths.csv` and `orgs.csv` have two more columns).

## Runs

- Arm `noprot`: the standard world (code default) with `--no-protect`.
  Seeds 1–10, 100,000 ticks, census every 500, recorded with `--out`,
  classified with `--classify` (default physics, `classes.csv`) and
  with `--classify --no-protect` (`classes_world.csv`; the
  single-genome harness has one organism, so the two should agree).
  Output `runs/e012/noprot/s<seed>/`. Runner
  `analysis/e012_no_protect.py`.
- Control: E007 seeds 1–10, windows 1–4 (ticks 0–100,000), the same
  seeds and physics with protection on. A run is bit-identical to its
  E007 seed until the first write that protection would have refused
  (that write then draws the write-noise RNG), so the arms share their
  start and diverge from there.

## Measures

Per run and per window of 25,000 ticks (as E007): population,
replicator-class bodies at the last census of the window, exact share
of births, canonical genotypes (`func_hash`) with ≥ 2 exact births,
the leading canonical genotype's share of exact births, parasite share
of exact births (E005's 2+-children test over the run), median absorb
count over replicator-class exact births of 21-byte bodies (E007's
trait measure); and, new, foreign writes landed (Σ`writes_foreign`
over the carriers that die in the window) and the share of deaths with
`overwritten` > 0. Whole run: E004's persistence table (`replicating` =
exact births by replicator-class parents in the last 5,000 ticks).
The same measures for E007 seeds 1–10 over windows 1–4, with
`writes_blocked` in place of `writes_foreign`, give the control.

## Predictions

1. **Persistence.** Replicating at tick 100,000 in at least 8 of 10
   seeds; median population at the end 700–1,100 (E007: 9 of 10, 936).
2. **No takeover.** In every surviving seed and every window from 2 on,
   the leading canonical genotype holds under 25% of the window's exact
   births, and at least 1,000 canonical genotypes have ≥ 2 exact births
   (E007 windows 2–10: 5–13% and 2,050–2,550).
3. **Diversity near the protected world.** For windows 2, 3 and 4, the
   median over surviving seeds of canonical genotypes with ≥ 2 exact
   births is 0.67–1.5 times E007's median for the same window.
4. **Foreign writes land at about the blocked rate.** Per seed, Σ
   `writes_foreign` to tick 100,000 is 0.5–2 times the same seed's
   E007 Σ`writes_blocked` to tick 100,000.
5. **Parasites persist.** The median over surviving seeds of the
   parasite share of exact births is 0.1–3% in each of windows 2–4.
6. **The absorb sweep happens as in E007.** The median absorb count is
   at least 112 in window 2 in at least 8 of the surviving seeds.
7. Exploratory, no threshold: which classes make the foreign writes
   (replicator-class, dependent, other); age at death of overwritten
   bodies against the rest; whether any genome with a foreign-write
   rate (`writes_foreign` / `executed`, pooled over carriers) above the
   99th percentile of the run reaches 1% of a window's exact births.

## Scoring H4

- **Refuted** if predictions 1 and 2 hold: without protection the
  world sustains many lineages, parasites among them. H4's refutation
  clause ("locality radius or energy cost already does protection's
  job") is then to be read as: `alloc`, which gives every replicator
  private room, does it, because no replicator needs to aim a write at
  a neighbour.
- **Supported** if in at least 6 of the surviving seeds one canonical
  genotype holds at least 50% of exact births in some window from 2 on
  and keeps it to the end, or replication ends in at least 6 seeds by
  tick 100,000.
- **Mixed** otherwise.

## Caveats, known now

- 100,000 ticks, not E007's 250,000. H4 predicts a collapse "within a
  short time", and E007 is stationary from window 2, so three
  stationary windows are the test; a slow takeover would be missed.
- The control is E007, run at commit fa60c1c without the new counters.
  The counters do not change the simulation (hash check above).
- One founder per run, as E007; seed 10 may lose its founder as it did
  there, before any foreign write lands.

## Results (2026-10-12)

Run on Linux from 482560f plus the uncommitted counter change
(`analysis/e012_no_protect.py`), 10 runs of 100,000 ticks, 40–75 s of
simulation and 100–165 s per classification each, about 10 minutes for
the batch; output in `runs/e012/` (`summary_e012.md` holds the
prediction checks; `summary_e004.md`, `summary.md`,
`summary_windows.md`, `windows.csv`, `writes_run.csv`, `control.csv`),
4.0 GB.

Checks: Σ`writes_foreign` = Σ`overwritten` over deaths and the living
at the end in 10 of 10 runs; `writes_blocked` is 0 in every run; the
control recomputation reproduces E007's `windows.csv` for windows 1–4
and the two `writes_blocked` sums quoted above. The arms share their
start: `births.csv` first differs from E007's at ticks 94–221 (seeds
1–9), where E007's first refused writes are. Seed 10 lost its founder
at tick 1,179 with no write refused, so it is E007's seed 10 exactly.
`classes.csv` and `classes_world.csv` are identical in 8 of 10 runs; in
seeds 2 and 7 three `inexact` genomes differ by 1–2 in `executed` and
none changes class (the harness holds the genome's children too, so a
write onto one can land; the design section's "one organism" was
loose). Seed 1 window 2 was rechecked from the raw files (2,291
canonical genotypes, leader 9.7%).

### Predictions

1. Replicating at 100,000 in ≥ 8 of 10, population 700–1,100:
   **confirmed**, 9 of 10 (seed 10 as in E007), median 941.
2. Leading canonical genotype under 25% and ≥ 1,000 canonical
   genotypes in every surviving seed-window from 2: **confirmed**; over
   27 seed-windows the leader holds at most 15.3% and the count is at
   least 2,047.
3. Canonical genotypes 0.67–1.5 times E007's for windows 2–4:
   **confirmed**, 1.06, 1.00, 1.08.
4. Foreign writes 0.5–2 times E007's refused writes per seed:
   **confirmed**, 0.69–1.06 in 9 of 9 (seed 10 has none in either arm).
5. Parasite share 0.1–3% in windows 2–4: **confirmed**, 0.88%, 0.81%,
   0.85%.
6. Absorb median ≥ 112 in window 2 in ≥ 8 surviving seeds:
   **confirmed**, 9 of 9 (116–124).
7. Exploratory:
   - Writers by class, pooled: 47% of landed writes by replicator-class
     bodies (40–62% per seed), 13% by dependent genomes (2.9% by
     parasites; 14.5% in seed 3, nearly all from one carrier of a
     21-byte one-shot parasite with 15,954 writes), 40% by the rest.
   - The writes are a few damaged bodies, not a strategy, and look the
     same with protection on and off. The median writer makes 2 writes
     (the design section's "about 17" was the mean); the top 1% of
     writers make 25–52% of all writes; 75–85% of writes are by bodies
     changed since birth. The heaviest single carriers made 4,500–40,000
     writes in a run in both arms.
   - Overwritten bodies are 0.8% of deaths in every window and die at a
     median age of 102 (83–135 by seed) against 17–18 for the rest:
     older bodies have had longer to be hit, so this says nothing about
     harm.
   - No genome with a foreign-write rate above its run's 99th
     percentile (31–43 genomes per seed) reaches 1% of a window's exact
     births; the most any reaches is 0.01%.

### Scoring H4

**Refuted**, by the registered rule: predictions 1 and 2 hold. Without
write protection the standard world keeps about 940 bodies, about 2,300
canonical genotypes with ≥ 2 exact births per window, a leader under
16% and parasites at 0.8–0.9% of exact births, as with protection on,
and the absorb sweep runs the same course. No genome takes over and
none with an elevated write rate spreads. The registered reading
applies: `alloc` does protection's job, because a replicator's copy
target is a region it was granted, so no replicator aims a write at a
neighbour. What protection refuses here is the collateral of broken
bodies, about 1.2–1.6 writes per tick, and letting it land changes
nothing measured.

### What this says

- Write protection is not what keeps this world diverse or its
  parasites alive. Coreworld's seeded replicators (PLAN §2) copied into
  memory nobody had reserved, since Core War has no allocation, and
  broke under noise and overwriting; give reproduction a private target
  and an overwriter has nothing to gain. That does not explain
  Amoeba-IV, which had an allocation op (`MALL`) and still never held
  two species (`literature.md` §2.4); its per-generation insertion of
  random code into memory is one difference from evo.
- Protection is still the default and nothing here argues for changing
  it: with it off, the world is the same and the record keeping is
  simpler (bodies change only by noise). It stays a Phase 4 ISA
  comparison switch.
- Parasitism here never needed protection: a fall-through parasite
  runs the host's code and writes into its own `alloc` region, so it
  writes nothing foreign either way.

### Caveats

- 100,000 ticks; a takeover slower than that would be missed (E007 is
  stationary from window 2 with protection on).
- Leader share and genotype counts are by `func_hash`; the leader of any
  class and the replicator-class leader were the same genome in every
  window.
- The control is E007, run at fa60c1c without the new counters (hash
  check in the design section).
