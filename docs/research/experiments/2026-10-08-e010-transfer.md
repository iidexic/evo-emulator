# E010: a transfer rule (the intruder gains what the host loses) (2026-10-08)

Status: design and predictions written 2026-10-08 before the
`--transfer-pct` flag exists and before any run. E010a (the gate: world
calibration, then the E009b injection test under the new rule) runs
first; E010 proper (the 250,000-tick search for an evolved defence) runs
only if the gate passes.

Bears on: H13 (a cost alone does not produce a defence on the E007
timescale), H14 (settled on its self-scan half by E009a/b: no owner-pays
rule tried makes a five-byte defence pay), H15 (new), E008 (the full
charge kills the entered host within one intruder cycle), E009b (only
7–14% of hosts are ever entered; an entry costs at most one pass; the
saving is an order of magnitude below the five-byte cost).

## Question

E009b closed the owner-pays line with an argument, not a run: a defence
is paid for on every copy, so it can only be selected if intrusion is
common and costly, and under a charge rule intrusion is neither. Few
hosts are entered because intruders gain nothing from entering (they
pay for their own instructions on host bytes as they would on debris),
and an entry costs the owner at most one pass because above the cliff
the owner dies and the intruder stops.

The transfer rule changes the intruder's side. Every instruction an
organism executes on another living organism's bytes moves energy from
the owner's store into the executor's. Intrusion then profits, entered
hosts are the intruder's food, intruders proliferate, and most hosts are
entered many times per lifetime. The host's loss is then a difference
in relative fitness that no store cap can hide, and a defence that
ejects the intruder has something to save on every cycle.

Two questions, in order. (1) At what N does the rule make intrusion
common without collapsing the host population? (2) At that N, is a
hand-built ejecting defence maintained by selection where the inert
control is lost? Only if both answer yes does the 250,000-tick search
for an evolved defence run.

## Rule

`--transfer-pct N` (`Config.transfer_pct`, default 0). In `Sim::step`,
after the op has run and the executor has paid its own full cost exactly
as under E007 physics (affordability unchanged: the executor's store
must cover the base cost and the tick budget must cover it), if the
byte at IP is owned by a living organism j other than the executor i:

    take = min(floor(cost * N / 100), energy_j)
    j.energy -= take           j.spent_m += take     j.paid_for_others_m += take
    room = max(0, store_cap_per_byte * len_i - energy_i)
    gain = min(take, room)
    i.energy += gain           i.gained_from_others_m += gain
    energy_out += take - gain

`gained_from_others_m` is a new `OrgStats` column (last in the CSV) and
enters the ledger: energy = born_with + absorb_gain + gained_from_others
− spent − upkeep − endowed. `paid_by_others_m` stays 0 under this rule.
The tick budget is debited by `cost` only (the transfer is not work).
Dead owners, debris and unowned bytes transfer nothing. N = 0 is
bit-identical to the E007 world. `--transfer-pct` together with
`--charge-owner` or `--charge-owner-pct` is rejected at the command
line.

Note on the range. For N ≤ 100 the net flows per instruction are those
of E009's split at the same N (owner −share, executor −(cost − share));
only the affordability check (full cost up front) and the store cap on
receipt differ. The informative range is N > 100, where foreign
execution nets the executor cost × (N − 100) / 100 per instruction. At
N = 200 an intruder earns one instruction's cost for every instruction
it runs on a host.

## Defences to test

The E009 self-scan (`self 1 ; swap C ; self 0 ; sub C ; jmpr A`, the
`trap`) sends a 21-byte intruder to its own start + 5 and spins a
one-byte `pad` on the `jmpr` until the pad starves. Under the transfer
rule the spin is a drain: the pad on the host's `jmpr` takes
cost_simple × N / 100 from the host per instruction and, at N ≥ 100,
never starves. The trap is therefore expected to be a liability here,
and is kept as a negative control.

The `eject` defence sends the intruder to a fixed far address instead:

    self 1 ; swap C ; self 0 ; sub C ; skipz A ; jmpa D

(6 bytes). A = executor start − address of the host's byte 0, so the
host (A = 0) skips the `jmpa` and falls through; any intruder jumps to
the absolute address in D. A pad's D is 0 (registers are zeroed at
birth and the pad executes nothing of its own before falling through),
so it lands at address 0; a 21-byte ancestor-like intruder carries its
loop constant in D (`lit D -4`) and lands at world_size − 4. Both are
the same neighbourhood on the torus, a sink at the ends of the address
space, which the design accepts and the runs must report (who lives
there). If the harness shows D not reliably far (an intruder whose D
points back at the host), the fallback is `zero D` before the `jmpa`
(7 bytes). The eject is verified in the harness before use: the host
alone is neutral against the K = 120 ancestor; a pad before the host
executes at most 6 host bytes and does not return in 3,000 ticks; the
21-byte intruder likewise; under N = 150 the host's loss per entry is at
most 6 × cost_simple × 1.5 = 9 units.

Lengths: `eject` 27 bytes (6 + 21); `inert` 27 bytes (six `zero D` +
21), the length control; `trap` 26 bytes; `resident` 21 bytes.

## Calibration (world)

Standard world, one founder, 50,000 ticks, seeds 1–3, N ∈ {100, 150,
200, 300}, plus the N = 0 reference (rerun under the new binary so the
CSV columns match). Measures over ticks 40,000–50,000 (the E007 world
after its absorb sweep):

- replicator-class exact births per 1,000 ticks, as a share of N = 0's;
- living replicator-class bodies at the last census;
- foreign-execution share of instructions;
- entered fraction: share of replicator-class deaths with
  `paid_for_others_m > 0`;
- entry cost: median `paid_for_others_m` among entered replicator-class
  deaths, and as a share of their `spent_m`;
- intruder profit: sum of `gained_from_others_m` over non-replicator-
  class deaths divided by the sum of their `spent_m`;
- `pad` (length ≤ 3) share of all births.

Harness rows first (3,000 ticks, 100 units each, pad placed immediately
before the host, K = 120 host): host exact births, host alive, pad
births, pad max energy, at each N; the trap host at N = 150 (expected:
drained); the eject host alone, with the pad, with the 21-byte intruder.

Arm rule: the gate's N is the lowest N whose median entered fraction is
≥ 50% and whose replicator-class birth rate is ≥ 25% of N = 0's (both
over seeds 1–3). If a second N one step lower also meets the entered
clause at ≥ 30%, it runs as a second arm. If no N meets both: the rule
either leaves intrusion rare (entered < 50% at N = 300: report and stop,
the transfer does not make intrusion common, H15 fails on its premise)
or collapses the hosts (births < 25% wherever entries are common:
report as parasite-driven collapse and stop; a lower N or a cap on the
transfer is a new design, not this one).

## Gate (E010a): injection at tick 25,000

As E009b: 50 copies of a test genome into free memory at tick 25,000,
runs to 50,000, exact births and the prefix measure per 2,500-tick bin,
living test bodies per census, test deaths. Rules: N = 0 and the chosen
N (and the second arm if any). Genomes: `eject`, `trap`, `inert`,
`resident`. Seeds 1–3. 4 genomes × (2 or 3 rules) × 3 seeds = 24 or 36
runs. Matching and readings as in E009b (`last > 1%` is the end tick of
the last 100-tick births bin with test share > 1%; rates in ticks since
injection; the prefix measure is the first six canonical (op, modifier)
pairs for `eject`/`inert`, five for `trap`, the canonical match for
`resident`).

## Predictions

1. **World persists.** At the chosen N, replicator-class births over
   ticks 40,000–50,000 are ≥ 25% of the N = 0 rate in 3 of 3 seeds (the
   calibration already requires this; restated so the gate's runs
   confirm it with the injected genomes present).
2. **Entries are common and profitable.** At the chosen N, ≥ 50% of
   replicator-class deaths after tick 25,000 were entered in 3 of 3
   seeds, and intruder profit (gained / spent over non-replicator
   deaths) is > 0.5 in 3 of 3.
3. **The eject is maintained.** At the chosen N, `eject`'s `last > 1%`
   is later than `inert`'s in 3 of 3 seeds, and `eject` has ≥ 1 living
   body at tick 50,000 in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds. Pass: E010 proper runs.
   Fail with 1 and 2 holding: the hand-built defence does not pay even
   when entries are common; before any search, compare the measured
   saving (median `paid_for_others_m` of entered hosts at the chosen N)
   with the six-byte cost (about 6% of a cycle, about 36 units) and
   record under H15.
4. **The trap is a liability.** At the chosen N, `trap` falls below 1%
   no later than `inert` in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds.
5. **N = 0 reference.** At N = 0, `eject` and `trap` are lost at
   `inert`'s rate (`last > 1%` within a factor of 2 of `inert`'s in
   ≥ 2 of 3 seeds), as in E009b.

## Caveats, known now

- The sink. Ejected intruders accumulate near address 0 and whoever
  lives there pays for everyone's defence. Report the entered fraction
  of bodies in the first and last patch separately.
- A profitable intruder is also a profitable host: a host's own
  intruders feed on it, but the host's children fall through into
  neighbours too. The calibration's `pad` share and intruder profit
  will say whether the rule turns the cloud into mutual grazing.
- N > 100 breaks the E009 equivalence, so the harness cliff is a new
  shape: an entered host now loses more than the intruder's cost. The
  harness rows are the first look; the world calibration decides.
- Three seeds, 100-tick bins, as before: the gate separates maintained
  from lost, not rates within a factor of 2.

### What this says (2026-10-08)

- **Profit did not raise intruder numbers, because intruders are not
  energy-limited.** In the harness the pad before a K = 120 host makes
  189 children in 3,000 ticks at every N including N = 0, where it
  gains nothing; its store is capped at 64 units for its one byte and
  its seed energy already exceeds that. In the world the `pad` share of
  births is 0.6–2.9% at every N and the transfer moves 1.2–1.4% of all
  energy spent (seed 1, ad hoc). Giving an intruder more energy than it
  can hold or use does not make more intruders; their numbers are set by
  placement and room, not by energy.
- **Each entry still ends the host within one pass.** Harness: the plain
  K = 120 host dies before its first child at every N ≥ 100 (host bytes
  run before death 531 at N = 100, 127 at N = 150 and 200, 63 at
  N = 300). In the world, foreign execution falls from 9.6% of
  instructions at N = 0 to 0.5–1.2% at N ≥ 100 because an entered host
  dies and its bytes become debris. The host's store (about 100–130
  units) is smaller than one pass of its own loop, so any owner-side
  cost per instruction above the store-cap overflow kills within a pass
  and any cost below it is invisible. This is the same cliff as E008 and
  E009's, now with the energy going somewhere instead of nowhere.
- **The entered fraction is geometric.** It is 12–14% here, 7–14% in
  E009b, and does not move with N. It is the chance that a body is
  placed where another body's fall-through reaches it. No per-
  instruction cost rule changes it, because the rule acts after the
  entry.
- **The eject works and is cheap** (host neutral at 139 births against
  inert's 139; the pad ejected to address 0 after 6 host bytes, the
  ancestor-like intruder to world_size − 4; host loss per entry 9 units
  at N = 150), so a working six-byte defence exists in this ISA. It has
  nothing to defend against: with entries rare and fatal, the saving
  per host lifetime is 0.13 × (one pass) at best.
- **Line closed.** Three cost rules (E008 full charge, E009 split, E010
  transfer) fail for the same structural reason: the host's store is
  one pass deep and intruder numbers are not energy-limited. A defence
  paid on every copy cannot be selected by an event that is rare and,
  when it happens, either costless or fatal. What would change this is
  not another per-instruction rule but a world where (a) an entered host
  survives many passes (a store several passes deep, which the Phase 1
  energy-per-organism decision and the 64-unit-per-byte cap set) and
  (b) intruder numbers are limited by energy (so that profit becomes
  proliferation). Both are changes to the physics, not to the cost
  rule, and belong to the Phase 3 energy-location question (PLAN §9).
  Recommended next: the recent-literature review already planned, then
  decide between the energy-location change and the Phase 4 BFF
  harness.
