# E009a: is the complete self-scan worth carrying? (2026-10-08)

Status: design and predictions written 2026-10-08 before the `--founder`
flag exists and before any run. Gate for E009: the 250,000-tick split
arms run only if some rule here keeps the defence.

Bears on: H14, the E009 calibration (a split charge of 10–50% leaves
entered hosts with the same exact children as unentered ones, band-ratio
medians 0.99–1.02 on seed 1; in the harness the charge is a cliff, free
below a threshold and lethal above), H13, PLAN §9 (several founders).

## Question

E009 was to search for a defence by mutation over 250,000 ticks. The
calibration says the cost that would pay for a defence is not visible in
the hosts' reproduction at any N tried. Rather than search for a defence
that may have nothing to pay for it, seed the finished defence and ask
whether selection keeps it. An invasion-from-rare test answers the fitness
question in 25,000 ticks: if the complete five-byte self-scan is lost at
every N, no charge of this kind makes five bytes worth carrying, and the
E009 search is closed before it starts. If it is kept or rises under some
rule, that rule is E009's arm and the search is worth running.

## Tool

`--founder HEX:COUNT`, repeatable, in `evo-core`. Each occurrence seeds
COUNT copies of the genome HEX, 100 units each, as the single founder is
seeded now. All founders from all `--founder` flags are placed evenly
around the ring in the order given (first flag's copies first), at
`world_size × j / total` for j = 0 … total − 1, offset so that no two
founders share a patch when total ≤ 128. Without the flag the run is
bit-identical to the current one-founder run (the E007 hashes at tick
20,000 of seed 1). `meta.json` records the founder list. The classifier
and the harness are unchanged.

## World and runs

The standard world. Every run has 110 founders: 100 copies of the
ancestor with K = 120 (E007's evolved host, byte 6 = 120) and 10 copies
of a test genome, so the test genome starts at 9.1% of bodies. Two test
genomes:

- `prefix`: the K = 120 ancestor with `self 1 ; swap C ; self 0 ; sub C ;
  jmpr A` (`33 52 13 4f 0c`) at byte 0; 26 bytes.
- `inert`: the K = 120 ancestor with five `zero D` at byte 0; 26 bytes,
  the same length cost and no defence (the body's `lit D -4` resets D).
  This is the control for "five extra bytes".

Four rules: N = 0 (E007 physics), `--charge-owner-pct 25`,
`--charge-owner-pct 50`, and `--charge-owner` (E008). Seeds 1–3 each.
24 runs of 25,000 ticks, census every 500, classified as usual. Runner
`analysis/e009a_invasion.py`, output `runs/e009a/<rule>_<genome>/s<seed>/`.

## Measures

Per 2,500-tick bin, over replicator-class exact births:

- the share of births whose genome carries the test prefix at byte 0,
  compared canonically (the `prefix` arms by the E009 prefix histogram,
  length 5; the `inert` arms by the same code given the inert sequence;
  `traits.track_genome` takes the prefix as a parameter).
- the share of births by bodies of 26 bytes or more (length-matched
  check on the same quantity).
- population, replicator-class bodies, foreign-execution share at the
  bin's last census, paid replicator deaths (where the rule charges).

Whole run: the test share in the last bin (ticks 22,500–25,000), and the
tick at which it last exceeds 1%, per run.

Harness, before the runs: `pad` before the `inert` K = 120 host under
each rule, for the table (expected: the E009 K = 120 row, since the inert
bytes do nothing).

## Predictions

1. Under N = 0 both test genomes are lost: the test share in the last
   bin is below 2% in 3 of 3 seeds for `prefix` and for `inert`, and the
   two decline at the same rate (the tick of last exceeding 1% within a
   factor of 2 between the genomes, seed by seed). Five bytes cost
   upkeep and copy time, and under E007 physics an intruder costs the
   host nothing to defend against.
2. Under N = 25 and N = 50 the `prefix` genome is also lost, below 2% in
   at least 2 of 3 seeds at each N, and not clearly slower than `inert`.
   This is the H14 reading of the calibration: entered hosts lose
   nothing measurable, so the defence has nothing to earn back.
3. Under the E008 rule the `prefix` genome does worse than `inert`, not
   better: a `pad` that spins on the jump is paid for entirely by the
   host and the host dies (E009 calibration: host dead at tick 17).
4. Exploratory: whether either 26-byte genome is lost faster than the
   E009 harness suggests (142 against 149 children alone); whether the
   `prefix` share, while it lasts, rises in bins where foreign execution
   is high.

If 2 fails (the prefix is kept or rises at some N), E009 runs with that N
as `hi`, and the prefix histogram is the main measure. If 1–3 hold, the
split-charge line is closed: a cost paid from a capped store does not
select a defence, and the next cost rule is a transfer (the intruder
gains what the host loses, PLAN §5.5), with this invasion test as its
first check.

## Caveats, known now

- 10 of 110 founders is one starting frequency; an invasion test at
  several frequencies would be better and is not done here.
- The background is the K = 120 ancestor, not an evolved E007
  population; the test genome competes against its own parent design.
- A 25,000-tick run is about 1,250 generations of a 20-tick cycle;
  neutral loss of a 9% genotype in a population of about 1,000 would not
  finish in that time, so a decline to below 2% in every seed is
  selection against, not drift.

### What this says (2026-10-08)

- **The test was decided by body length in a colonisation race, not by
  the defence.** Both 26-byte genomes, the one with the self-scan and the
  one with five inert bytes, go from 9.1% of founders to below 1% of
  exact births within 400–1,700 ticks under every rule, and at the same
  rate within the resolution of the measure. In the first 2,000 ticks
  the population grows from 110 to about 900 bodies, a race in which a
  5-byte longer body copies more slowly every generation; the harness
  gap of 142 against 149 children (5%) compounds over the 50–80
  generations of the race to the factor of 10 seen. Prediction 1's
  narrow failure (ratio 2.17 against 2 in one seed) is noise around
  "the same rate".
- **The defence had nothing to defend against.** At tick 0 there are no
  intruders; fall-through bodies arise by mutation and foreign execution
  reaches its E007 level only after the test genomes are gone. So the
  prefix's benefit, if any, never had a chance to be paid, and
  prediction 2 holding here does not close the split-charge line; it
  says that a five-byte defence cannot invade during a growth phase with
  no parasites, which was not the question.
- **What it does establish.** Five extra bytes cost about 5% per
  generation on the K = 120 host in the harness and are lost at a rate
  consistent with that in the world. Any defence of that length must
  earn back at least that much, every generation, from intrusions.
- **Next.** The fair test injects the finished defence into an
  established, parasitised population: E009b, a `--inject TICK:HEX:COUNT`
  flag, the standard one-founder world to tick 25,000, then copies of
  the test genome placed in free memory, with a 21-byte K = 120 resident
  injected the same way as the control for the injection itself
  (`experiments/2026-10-08-e009b-late-injection.md`).
