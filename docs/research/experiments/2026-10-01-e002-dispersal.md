# E002: dispersal arms (2026-10-01)

Status: set up, not yet run. Predictions below are written before the full
runs (H10 in `../hypotheses.md`).

Bears on: H10 (dispersal sustains replication), H5 (turnover).

## Question

E001 found that every child lands in its parent's patch: `alloc` takes the
nearest free run, and locality 512 is small next to a 4,096-byte patch. The
colony overshoots that one patch's income, crashes, and loafers outlast
replicators through the crashes. Does getting children into other patches
keep replication going?

## Arms

All at `Config::default()` except as listed, ancestor K = 64, E = 16 seeded
at world/2 with 100 energy. 10 seeds (1–10) x 50,000 ticks, census every
500 ticks. `--patch N` scales per-patch income and pool cap by N / 4,096, so
energy per byte and total sunlight stay the same in every arm.

| arm | change | which E001 option |
|---|---|---|
| control | none (patch 4,096) | baseline |
| p2048 | patch 2,048 | smaller patches, same total income |
| p1024 | patch 1,024 | same, locality = patch / 2 |
| p512 | patch 512 | "many small patches", patch = locality |
| far | `alloc` takes the farthest fitting run within locality | alloc prefers farther space |
| far_p1024 | far + patch 1,024 | combination |

The E001 options "patch 512 with the same total income" and "a 1D world
with many small patches" turned out to be the same axis (patch size at
constant energy density), so they are run as a sweep, 2,048 down to 512.

## Metrics (from the CLI census, summarized by the script)

- extinct: runs where every organism died.
- still replicating: not extinct and a birth in the last 5,000 ticks.
- last birth tick: last census row in which the birth count rose (500-tick
  resolution; an extinct run also gets a row at its extinction tick).
- births: cumulative at the end.
- alive, patches occupied, active fraction: means over the second half
  (ticks 25,001–50,000). Patches occupied counts patches holding at least
  one living body start. Active fraction is the share of organisms alive at
  a census that ran `divide` during the preceding 500 ticks; 1 minus it is
  roughly the loafer share (a newborn counts as inactive until it divides).
- Medians across seeds for everything except the counts.

## Predictions (written before the full runs)

1. control: replication stops in most seeds, at most 3 of 10 still
   replicating; patches occupied stays around 1–3 (E001 run 4).
2. p512: extinct in all 10 seeds, early. A 512-byte patch gets 32 energy per
   tick, below one ancestor's draw (about 35 per tick, §5.3.3 budget), and
   absorb costs energy even when the pool is empty. A 1,000-tick plumbing
   check at seed 1 already went extinct at tick 80, so this one is not a
   blind prediction.
3. p1024 and p2048: more patches occupied than control and more seeds still
   replicating; p1024 best of the sweep (at least 5 of 10). A crash in one
   patch no longer takes the whole colony, and locality 512 = patch / 2
   lets a child reach the next patch from the back half of its parent's.
4. far: more spread than control but less than p1024; children land about
   490 bytes away, which only crosses a 4,096-byte patch boundary from the
   last eighth of a patch.
5. far_p1024: highest patches occupied of all arms.
6. Active fraction tracks "still replicating": arms that stall end up mostly
   loafers.

## Caveats

- Smaller patches also mean a smaller pool cap per patch (scaled with size),
  so less buffer against a crash. Spread and buffer change together.
- `alloc` tries the forward candidate before the backward one at each
  distance, in both modes. Under `alloc_far` this biases children forward,
  so the colony may drift one way around the ring.
- Seeds 1–10 give rough rates, not tight ones; a 2-of-10 difference is noise.

## Setup

- Code: `Config::alloc_far`, CLI flags `--patch`, `--alloc-far`,
  `--locality`, and two new census columns (`patches_occ`, `active`).
- Runner: `python scripts/e002_dispersal.py` builds, runs all arms in
  parallel, writes raw CSVs to `runs/e002/` (gitignored), and prints the
  summary table. `--summarize-only` re-reads existing output.
- Found while building: `alloc` only range-checked a region's last byte, so
  a backward region could start outside locality. Fixed in its own commit
  before this one, so E002 and E001 differ in that too.
- Plumbing check after the predictions were written: 2 seeds x 2,000 ticks.
  It caught two metric bugs (active fraction above 1; runs extinct before
  the first census left no row), both fixed. It also showed p1024 extinct
  in both seeds by tick 100 while far_p1024 survived: with nearest-free
  `alloc` the first child shares its parent's 64-per-tick patch and both
  starve. Prediction 3 is likely wrong; it stays as written.
